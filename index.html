The Campus Problem

At Limkokwing University and schools across Sierra Leone, three critical challenges are affecting the learning environment:

No Tissue Holders in Classrooms

Students and lecturers lack basic hygiene infrastructure. Classrooms have no tissue dispensers, forcing reliance on shared cloth or personal supplies — unhygienic and inconvenient.

Campus Issue #1

Plastic Waste Overflow

Bottle caps, water bottles, and plastic containers pile up on campus with no recycling system. Freetown produces 96,000 tons of plastic waste annually — our campus contributes significantly.

Campus Issue #2

Bare Learning Spaces

Classrooms and libraries lack decorative, functional products. Learning environments are sterile and uninspiring, affecting student morale and creativity — a national education infrastructure gap.

Campus Issue #3

How We Transform Waste

From discarded bottle caps and paper cartons to beautiful classroom essentials

01

Collect

We gather discarded Sierra Leone bottle caps, plastic bottles, and paper cartons from campus hostels, cafeterias, and classrooms. Every cap is a potential product.

02

Clean & Sort

All materials are thoroughly washed, sanitized, and sorted by color and type. Bottle caps are organized by shade to create beautiful patterns.

03

Design & Assemble

Our team arranges bottle caps into patterns, attaches them to paper carton bases, and adds finishing touches. Each piece is handcrafted with care.

04

Deliver to Classrooms

Finished products are delivered back to campus classrooms, libraries, and study halls — transforming waste into essential learning tools.

Our Mission

EcoGlow Creations was born from real problems we face every day at Limkokwing University. Our classrooms lack tissue holders. Plastic bottles and caps overflow from bins. Learning spaces feel bare and uninspiring.

We asked: "What if we could solve all three problems at once?" By collecting campus plastic waste and transforming it into tissue boxes, decorative mirrors, and utility products — we create value from waste while improving our learning environment.

This model can scale across all Sierra Leone schools, where 64% lack water access and 48% have inadequate sanitation facilities. We're turning environmental liability into educational asset.
